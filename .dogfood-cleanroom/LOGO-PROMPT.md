# slopvac logo prompts

The name is a play on **shopvac**: the squat wet/dry workshop vacuum that sucks up
whatever mess is on the floor. `slopvac` sucks the slop out of prose. The joke
should read instantly and without a caption.

Aim for something that survives at 32px in a favicon and at 400px in a README.

---

## Primary prompt

> A flat vector logo for a developer tool called "slopvac". The subject is a squat
> cylindrical wet/dry shop vacuum, seen three-quarter front, with a wide ribbed
> hose curling up from the side and a chunky nozzle at the end. Instead of dust,
> the nozzle is inhaling a short stream of text fragments: em dashes, semicolons,
> and a couple of small word-shaped blocks, tumbling as they get pulled in. They
> should be unmistakably typography rather than debris.
>
> Style: bold flat vector, thick uniform outlines, no gradients, no shadows, no
> texture. Three colours plus the background. Confident geometric shapes, generous
> negative space, slightly rounded corners. The kind of mark that would sit on a
> GitHub README or a PyPI page.
>
> Palette: a saturated industrial orange-red for the vacuum body, near-black for
> the outlines and the hose, off-white background, one muted grey for the text
> fragments being swallowed.
>
> Composition: centred, roughly square, works as a small icon. No lettering, no
> wordmark, no caption. Clean vector edges, transparent background.

---

## Variant A: icon only, maximum simplicity

For the favicon and the CLI splash. Drop the vacuum body entirely.

> A minimal flat vector app icon: a single vacuum nozzle pointed up and to the
> left, with three em dashes and one semicolon spiralling into its mouth. Two
> colours only: near-black linework on a warm orange field. Thick strokes, heavy
> geometric simplification, extreme negative space, legible at 16 pixels. Rounded
> square icon format. No text, no wordmark.

---

## Variant B: the wordmark lockup

> A horizontal logo lockup for a command-line tool. On the left, a small flat
> vector shop vacuum with its hose curling into a spiral. On the right, the
> lowercase wordmark "slopvac" set in a heavy geometric sans-serif, tightly
> tracked, all lowercase, no descender flourishes.
>
> The hose spiral should visually feed into the first letter, as though the word
> is being drawn out of the machine.
>
> Flat vector, two colours: near-black type, industrial orange for the vacuum.
> Transparent background. Balanced optical weight between mark and type.

---

## Variant C: the before/after gag

For a social card or the docs hero, where there is room for a beat.

> A flat vector illustration in a wide banner format. On the left, a messy cloud
> of typographic debris: em dashes, semicolons, ellipses, and small grey word
> blocks scattered loosely. In the centre, a squat orange shop vacuum with its
> hose reaching left into the mess. On the right, the same area is empty except
> for three or four tidy word blocks arranged in a clean baseline row.
>
> The visual argument is left-to-right: clutter in, clarity out.
>
> Bold flat vector, thick outlines, no gradients. Industrial orange, near-black,
> one grey, off-white background. No text or caption.

---

## Things to steer away from

Worth stating explicitly, because image models reach for these unprompted:

- **A magnifying glass.** Every linter logo has one. The vacuum is the whole idea;
  do not dilute it.
- **A robot, a brain, or a neural-network motif.** The tool removes the machine
  fingerprint. Putting a machine on the box argues the opposite.
- **A broom, a mop, or a spray bottle.** Wrong appliance, and the pun dies.
- **Gradients, glows, bevels, or 3D renders.** They break at icon size and date
  the mark immediately.
- **Actual dirt, dust clouds, or crumbs.** What it removes is language, so the
  intake has to read as typography.
- **A red circle-slash or any prohibition sign.** The tool measures and reports;
  it does not forbid.
- **Trade dress.** Do not reproduce the Shop-Vac brand's own livery or logo. The
  pun is on the generic appliance, and the mark must not read as that company's.
